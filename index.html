THE STORY BEHIND THE SPIRAL

Located on Manhattan’s Upper East Side, the Solomon R. Guggenheim Museum is an internationally recognized institution dedicated to modern and contemporary art. Yet its story began long before its famous spiral building existed.

In 1937, American art collector and philanthropist Solomon R. Guggenheim established the Solomon R. Guggenheim Foundation to promote art and educate the public. With the guidance of artist and advisor Hilla Rebay, Guggenheim developed a passion for non-objective art, a form of abstraction that does not depict recognizable objects or scenes.

Their vision became a reality in 1939 with the opening of the Museum of Non-Objective Painting in Manhattan. The museum introduced audiences to experimental artists, including Vasily Kandinsky, whose work challenged traditional ideas about artistic representation.

As the collection expanded, the need for a permanent home became apparent. In 1943, Rebay commissioned renowned architect Frank Lloyd Wright to design a revolutionary museum unlike any other. The institution was renamed the Solomon R. Guggenheim Museum in 1952, honoring its founder.

On October 21, 1959, Wright’s remarkable building opened on Fifth Avenue. Rather than conventional rectangular galleries, the museum featured a continuous spiral ramp surrounding a magnificent central atrium.

Today, the Guggenheim represents far more than a collection of artworks. Its architecture transforms the experience of viewing art, making the building itself one of New York City’s most distinctive artistic achievements.

BEYOND THE ART: A MISSION TO INSPIRE

The Solomon R. Guggenheim Museum strives to make modern and contemporary art meaningful and accessible to audiences around the world. As part of the Solomon R. Guggenheim Foundation, its mission centers on collecting, preserving, and interpreting art while encouraging innovation, cultural exchange, and public education.

Through exhibitions, research, educational programs, and international collaborations, the Guggenheim seeks to deepen people's understanding and appreciation of artistic expression. Its mission extends beyond displaying masterpieces: the museum encourages visitors to engage with unfamiliar ideas, discover different cultural perspectives, and reconsider conventional ways of viewing art.

Innovation, education, accessibility, and cultural exchange are central themes reflected in the institution's work. Its educational initiatives create opportunities for audiences of different ages and backgrounds to learn about art, while its international network connects artistic traditions and contemporary voices from around the world.

Although the foundation does not publish a separate overarching vision statement on its main About Us page, its mission suggests a broader aspiration: a world in which art brings people together, encourages curiosity, and continually expands our understanding of creativity and culture.
