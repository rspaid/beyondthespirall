01 / The origins

A museum that
changed the rules.

The Guggenheim began with one collector's ambition: to bring groundbreaking abstract art to a wider public.

In 1937, philanthropist Solomon R. Guggenheim established the Solomon R. Guggenheim Foundation. Guided by artist and advisor Hilla Rebay, he supported non-objective art—art that does not depict recognizable subjects. The foundation opened the Museum of Non-Objective Painting in 1939.

Renamed the Solomon R. Guggenheim Museum in 1952, the institution found its iconic home in 1959. Architect Frank Lloyd Wright designed a bold alternative to traditional galleries: a continuous spiral ramp wrapped around a soaring central atrium. The building transformed how visitors move through and experience art.

02 / Mission & vision

Art should open
new ways of seeing.

The Guggenheim Foundation works to collect, preserve, and interpret modern and contemporary art—and to invite audiences into meaningful encounters with it.

Through exhibitions, scholarship, and educational programs, the museum fosters understanding of art across cultures and generations. The four themes below summarize values reflected in its work; they are interpretive headings, not a separately published official vision statement.

01

Innovation

Championing experimentation in art, exhibitions, and architecture.

02

Education

Helping visitors engage with art through learning and interpretation.

03

Access

Creating opportunities for varied audiences to encounter art.

04

Exchange

Connecting artistic perspectives from different places and traditions.
